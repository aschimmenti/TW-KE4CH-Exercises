Mahmoud Darwish (1941–2008) was born in al-Birwa, Western Galilee, into a landowning family. During the 1948 Nakba, his village was destroyed by the IDF and his family fled to Lebanon before returning to settle in the Acre area. Despite Israel's 1952 citizenship law, Darwish and his family were classified as residents rather than citizens. He published his first poetry collection, Wingless Birds, at 19, and became editor of Al Jadid, the literary periodical of the Israeli Communist Party, of which he was a member. In 1970 he left Israel to study at Lomonosov Moscow State University, then moved to Cairo to work for al-Ahram. Joining the PLO in 1973 barred him from re-entering Israel. He edited Shu'un Filistiniyya in Beirut and was elected to the PLO Executive Committee in 1987, resigning in 1993 in opposition to the Oslo Accords. He subsequently settled in Ramallah. Over his lifetime Darwish published more than 30 volumes of poetry and eight books of prose. His 1964 poem "Identity Card," with its repeated line "Write down: I am an Arab," spread rapidly across the Arab world after a public reading in Nazareth. His 1966 "To My Mother" became an unofficial Palestinian anthem. Following the 1967 war he committed to documenting Palestinian displacement, and the 1982 Lebanon War produced the political poems Qasidat Beirut and Madih al-zill al'ali. Later collections include Ward aqall (1986), Bed of a Stranger (2000), Why Did You Leave the Horse Alone? (2000), State of Siege (2003), and Mural (2006). His prose work Memory for Forgetfulness addresses the 1982 siege of Beirut. He also wrote the 1988 Palestinian Declaration of Independence. His cited literary influences included Arthur Rimbaud, Allen Ginsberg, and the Iraqi poets Abd al-Wahhab Al-Bayati and Badr Shakir al-Sayyab. Darwish died on 9 August 2008 in Houston, three days after heart surgery, and was buried in Ramallah following a state funeral attended by thousands. Palestinian President Mahmoud Abbas declared three days of national mourning.
