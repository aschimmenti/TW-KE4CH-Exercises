Letizia Battaglia

Photographer & Photojournalist · 5 March 1935 – 13 April 2022

Early Life and Career

Letizia Battaglia was born and died in Palermo. She began photographing at the age of 34 in 1969, initially collaborating with the Palermo newspaper L'Ora as the only woman among male colleagues. After a period in Milan, she returned to Palermo in 1974 and founded the photographic agency Informazione fotografica with Franco Zecchin, frequented by photographers including Josef Koudelka and Ferdinando Scianna.

The Anti-Mafia Lens

From 1974 onward she documented the Palermo Mafia's violence systematically, photographing crime scenes and murder victims to inform public opinion. Her archive records the dominance of the Corleonesi clan; among her most significant images are photographs of Mafia tax collectors alongside Giulio Andreotti, which were admitted as evidence in a trial, and the photograph of Sergio Mattarella holding the body of his brother, President of the Sicilian Region Piersanti Mattarella, taken on 6 January 1980 — the day she was the first photoreporter to arrive at the scene. Her 1980 photograph of a young girl with a ball in the Cala neighbourhood circulated internationally. She withdrew from photographing Mafia crime after the assassination of Judge Giovanni Falcone on 23 May 1992.

Broader Work and Legacy

Her work extended beyond anti-Mafia documentation to encompass Palermo's daily life, its women and children, its neighbourhoods and traditions, consistently rendered in black and white. In the 1980s she established the Laboratorio d'If, where a generation of Palermitan photographers trained. In 2017 she founded the Centro Internazionale di Fotografia in Palermo.

Recognition

In 1985 she became the first European woman to receive the W. Eugene Smith Award in New York, shared with Donna Ferrato. She also received the Mother Johnson Achievement for Life award in 1999. Her work was exhibited internationally, including at the Centre Pompidou in Paris.

Political Life

She was politically active from the late 1980s, serving as a councillor and assessore in Palermo under Mayor Orlando, and was elected to the Sicilian Regional Assembly in 1991 with La Rete. She had three daughters from her first marriage and died of cancer on 13 April 2022 at the age of 87.
